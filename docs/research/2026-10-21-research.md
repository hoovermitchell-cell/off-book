# Research, 2026-10-21

Four parallel research passes (Claude Code subagents) ahead of the planning
grill. Confidence: **H** primary source or live probe, **M** secondary or
partly verified, **L** inference or unverified. Re-check anything marked M or L
before depending on it.

## Engine and stack

- **Stockfish is still the strongest public engine (H).** Stockfish 19
  (released 2026-09-05) leads CCRL 40/15 (list of 2026-10-16) at 3650, but
  Reckless, PlentyChess, pawnocchio and Torch are within about 11 Elo. It won
  the TCEC Season 29 Superfinal against Reckless (exact score M). Leela is
  competitive only on GPU. Above about 3000 Elo the difference is irrelevant
  for this audience.
  [CCRL](https://computerchess.org.uk/ccrl/4040/) ·
  [SF19](https://stockfishchess.org/blog/2026/stockfish-19/)
- **Browser builds (H):** `nmrugg/stockfish.js` v19.0.1 ships full (about
  94 MB), lite multi-threaded (about 1.6 MB, needs COOP/COEP headers), lite
  single-threaded (about 1.7 MB, no headers), and an ASM-JS fallback. Its README
  recommends lite single-threaded. `lichess-org/stockfish-web` is the advanced
  option. [stockfish.js](https://github.com/nmrugg/stockfish.js)
- **Chromebook speed: not verified (L).** No benchmarks found. The guess is depth
  14 to 18 in 1 to 2 s per position for lite single-threaded. The spike
  measures it.
- **Licenses (H):** Stockfish GPLv3, chessground GPL-3.0+, chessops GPL-3.0+,
  chess.js BSD-2, react-chessboard MIT, cm-chessboard MIT (some piece sets
  CC BY-NC-SA). Shipping GPL code to browsers is distribution, so the app is
  GPL-family and its source must be offered.
- **Maia (M):**
  - Maia-1: GPL, lc0 weights for 1100 to 1900; needs lc0, no known browser
    build.
  - Maia-2: MIT, Python only.
  - Maia-3: AGPL, 5M/23M/79M models, Elo inputs for both players.
  - An unofficial ONNX export of Maia-3 runs in onnxruntime-web: 5M model is
    10.8 MB fp16. [maia3-onnx](https://huggingface.co/bqrio/maia3-onnx)
  - Maia-3's valid Elo range is **not verified**; Maia-1's floor is 1100.
  - Depth-limited Stockfish matches human moves only 35 to 40% of the time
    (Maia team). [Maia](https://maiachess.com/blog/introducing_maia)
- **Openings data (H):** `lichess-org/chess-openings`, CC0 TSV with eco, name,
  pgn, uci, epd. [repo](https://github.com/lichess-org/chess-openings)

## Lichess and Chess.com

- **Lichess API (H, live probes):**
  - Profile, game export and cloud eval are anonymous and CORS-open.
  - Game export takes `evals`, `clocks`, `opening`, `accuracy` and `division`
    flags; evals only exist on analysed games.
  - Official guidance is one request at a time, and wait a minute after a 429.
    [API tips](https://lichess.org/page/api-tips)
- **Lichess opening explorer now returns 401 without a token (H).** A personal
  token reportedly fixes it (M). Do not depend on it from the client.
- **Lichess puzzle database (H):** 6,157,341 puzzles, CC0, about 307 MB
  compressed. Fields include Rating, Themes, Popularity and **OpeningTags**,
  which is what makes opening-linked puzzles a plain filter.
  [database](https://database.lichess.org)
- **Chess.com Published-Data API (H):**
  - Read-only: profiles, ratings, and monthly PGN archives (no engine evals).
  - Serial requests only, data refreshes every 12 to 24 hours, CORS observed
    open but undocumented.
  - Asks for a User-Agent with contact info, which browsers can't set.
  [docs](https://www.chess.com/news/view/published-data-api)
- **Ages (H):** Lichess accounts 15+, or a kid account created by a teacher or
  parent. Chess.com 13+ with parental consent below the age of majority.

## Teaching and gamification

- **Evidence (M/H):**
  - Southwick et al. 2026 (44,213 Chess.com players): deliberate-practice
    activities gave about 3.6x the learning per unit of time of plain play.
    The abstract doesn't say which activities.
  - The EEF school-chess trial (4,009 pupils) found no effect on maths, reading
    or science, so make no academic claims.
  - Woodpecker and "analyze every loss" are plausible but unproven.
- **Gamification harm (M):** leaderboards are the most-cited element in
  research on gamification's negative effects. Prefer effort-based rewards,
  personal bests, team goals, and streaks with freezes or a weekly rhythm.
- **LLM coaching (M):** published work consistently finds fluent but wrong
  commentary. The workable pattern is that the engine and detector code
  produce facts and the LLM only puts them into words. A checker rejects
  squares or pieces that aren't on the board, with a template fallback.
- **The gap (M):** Lichess, Chess.com and Chessbook cover solo practice.
  ChessKid and Chessity have school or coach features. A layer driven by each
  student's own games across both sites is what's least served.

## School deployment

- **Privacy (M):**
  - The amended COPPA rule's compliance date was 2026-04-22; the proposed
    school-authorization exception was not adopted.
  - FERPA binds schools, not vendors, unless student records are handed over.
  - NC DPI expects vendors to sign its Data Confidentiality and Security
    Agreement before districts share student data.
  - Storing no personal data avoids nearly all of this.
  [DPI agreement](https://www.dpi.nc.gov/data-confidentiality-and-security-agreement-online-service-providers-and-public-school-units-updated/open)
- **School IT (M):** whether chess sites are blocked varies by district; ask IT
  to allow the app's domain. Google sign-in for under-18 users needs admin
  approval.
- **LLMs and minors (H):** Anthropic requires safeguards and AI disclosure for
  products serving minors.
  [policy](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9307344-responsible-use-of-anthropic-s-models-guidelines-for-organizations-serving-minors)
- **Hosting (H):**
  - Cloudflare Pages `_headers` can set COOP/COEP on static assets.
  - GitHub Pages can't set those headers.
  - Vercel Hobby is non-commercial only.
  - A Cloudflare Worker (free tier 100k requests/day) is enough for any later
    proxy.
- **Accessibility (M):** DOJ's ADA Title II rule requires WCAG 2.1 AA
  (deadlines 2027 to 2028). Keyboard-operable board, moves announced in a
  live region, never color alone.
