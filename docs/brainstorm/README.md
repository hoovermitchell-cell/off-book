## What Claude would bring to Mitch first

1. **"Without fail" (decision 14, open).** The research's option C: each position correct on three
   separate days, plus two clean runs of the whole line on different days before alternatives
   unlock. It tests the line in order, which matches "execute the main line without fail."
2. **Shared Chromebooks (Codex risk 3).** Device-only progress breaks if two students share a
   browser profile. Worth one question to the school before building stages, because the answer
   decides whether a simple local profile picker is needed.
3. **Prep-boundary review (Codex's top pick).** The review step for over-the-board games: replay the
   game against the staged lines, mark who left prep first and whether it cost anything, end with
   one lesson to revisit. It completes the core loop from decision 1.

## Worth keeping in view

- A wrong but legal move typed from a scoresheet can get a convincing analysis (Codex risk 1).
  Ask the student to confirm the record before analysis; consider checkpoints during entry.
- The real-match stage measures opportunity as much as skill (Codex risk 2). Show "not yet
  played in a real game" rather than treating it as a failure.
- "Pin one position for next club" is a gentle reason to come back that fits Earned, not awarded.
